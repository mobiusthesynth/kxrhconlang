Grammar

Words without any suffixes are by default nouns.
A noun without a suffix that follows after another noun is a compounding noun, fusing itself and the previous noun to make a semantically novel compound noun.
Descriptors are a class of word that can be used as adjectives, adverbs, or determiners.
Sentences are structured in a rigid, head-initial SVO order.
The order of qualifiers being attached to nouns or relative clauses is: COMPOUNDING NOUN > ADJECTIVE/ADVERB > APPOSITIONED NOUN CLAUSE > RELATIVE CLAUSE > POSSESSIVE NOUN > PREPOSITIONAL CLAUSE.

Suffixes

Vowel suffixes always generate syllable breaks unless attached to a simple root.
Words cannot have more than one vowel suffix or more than one consonant suffix.
Consonant suffixes always come after the vowel suffix.
Consonant suffixes are represented in script by replacing the <gh> with itself in the preceding vowel suffix.
A suffix that ends in an <h> has the <h> omitted in pronunciation and in romanization, though it does still remain in script.
A vowel suffix gains a <gh> before the vowel when being attached to an open syllable.
Consonant suffixes cannot be added to the descriptor suffix.
Unless the preceding vowel suffix differentiates function by if a consonant suffix is attached, a consonant suffix either adds a pronomial possessive to a noun or adds a pronomial subject to a verb.

Simple roots

Simple roots are in and of themselves words.
Simple roots must have a vowel suffix attached.
Simple roots that do not have a definition for a specific word type cannot be used to form that type of word no matter what suffix gets attached to them.

Function words

Function words can behave as pronouns, determiners, prepositions, stranded prepositions, or conjunctions depending on context.
Function words never take any suffixes.
